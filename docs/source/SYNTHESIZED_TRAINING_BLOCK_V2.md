# Twelve-week training block — V2 review draft

Prepared September 21, 2026. Start with sections 1, 6–10 and your matching weekly card. The detailed evidence and verification remain available for review.

## 1. Assessment and what makes this a stronger program

**Coaching design grade: A, provisionally.** This is a complete review draft, not a scientifically scored A+ or an approved/achieved training outcome. The important weaknesses have concrete repairs: progressive power practice, rep-specific pull-up preparation, workable arm progression, honest clocks and a real deferred-test slot. The remaining test of quality is whether the dose fits your actual recovery while the priority lifts improve.

The weakest assumption is that the new ballistic and unilateral work can remain technically crisp and low-fatigue alongside your actual cycling. Kettlebell/throw proficiency, throwing space, machine configuration, transit time and individual recovery remain unconfirmed. First-two-week observations determine whether you hold or reduce the entry doses; they do not authorize skipped ramps or invented capacity.

Strengths: stable priority lifts and full rests; two lower strength days; controlled new leg volume; relevant five-/six-rep practice; shoulder/adductor work; clear impact gates; and adaptable testing. Remaining tradeoffs: very small high-impact doses may not deliver four inches of jump improvement; weekly arm variation slows same-exercise feedback; Sunday power follows a long ride; and a combined test day can fatigue later tests. These are managed openly rather than scored away.

An A+ personal fit would mean you can repeatedly execute the program, maintain prescribed cycling and normal tissue response, improve comparable priority sets, demonstrate repeatable power quality, and adjust promptly when the evidence from your log disagrees with the table. More exercises or more weekly increases would not establish that fit.

Preserved profile: 38-year-old male, 6 ft, about 170 lb, lean, over ten years of strength/cycling training, long femurs/shorter torso, FTP 229 as context. Strong lifting history does not establish sprint/landing readiness. Recent impact exposure is limited; familiar mild post-run adductor soreness has not altered gait. Bench 205×2, squat held/toward 245×2, clean DL 450×2 and bodyweight 170±2 are tracked, non-binding criteria. No performance is guaranteed.

## 3. Evidence and practitioner principles

Heavy specific practice, adequate recovery, purposeful accessory work and consistent progression support this structure. Exact KB, throw, unilateral and arm doses are coaching decisions under your constraints, not proven optimal prescriptions.

- **Mike Boyle influence:** unilateral strength, supported rows and purposeful power/shoulder work. Your required primary lifts still govern; this is not a claim that Boyle endorses the split. [Example adult-program material](https://files.inspire360.tech/bodybyboyleonline/65ff001f38876b11e807973c.pdf).
- **Pavel influence:** crisp repeatable strength/power practice, full recovery and one coherent progression. No incompatible full programs are layered together. [StrongFirst, The 3–5 Method Revisited](https://www.strongfirst.com/the-3-5-method-revisited/).
- **Dan John influence:** stable movements, simple progress records and a small number of priorities. [Long-form training interview](https://legionathletics.com/muscle-for-life-episode-787-dan-john-interview/).
- **Layne Norton influence:** measurable progression, consistent execution and response-led adjustment. Rep-range progression fits this approach; it is not a proprietary or uniquely validated method. [Training FAQ](https://biolayne.com/faq/part-h-training/).

The practitioners inform judgment; the research ledger supplies evidence and limitations. Neither substitutes for your response to training.

## 5. Training architecture and combined stress

Normal-week strength architecture: 18 compound press sets, 9 vertical pulls, 6 horizontal pulls, 3 squat doubles and 2 DL doubles. Power/ramp sets do not inflate these totals. Add only one work pair of reverse lunges Monday and one supported single-leg hinge Friday. Copenhagen remains a separate three-pair dose on each lower day. This keeps meaningful lower strength on exactly two days, while acknowledging that jumps, swings, cleans and cycling still create lower-body stress.

| Day | Priority sequence | Added work and where fatigue matters |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday PM | Brief one-arm clean → heavy dip → paused bench → supported row | Clean 2×2/side plus a light rehearsal, biceps/core/ER and one easy farmer carry. ≥6 h after actual ride finish. If lower fatigue compromises clean technique, select the supported upper-body speed-row fallback. |
| Monday PM | Swing → fresh pull-up → conventional DL → moderate OHP | Swing 2×5 plus light rehearsal; lunge 1×6/side, Copenhagen 3×6/side and Pallof. Swing/pull-up grip costs are monitored before DL. |
| Wednesday PM | Impact first; throw → heavy OHP → pull-up → one-arm supported row | Throws 2×3/side plus 1/side rehearsal, counted once as power and rotation. Face pull, biceps, abs, one easy suitcase carry. Throw quality must not fatigue OHP. |
| Friday AM | Impact first; existing maximal jump supplies power where scheduled → squat → dip → pull-up | Cleans only W2–4, reduced familiar version W6. Single-leg hinge 1×6/side after main lifts, Copenhagen, prone Y and rotation. Optional ride only afterwards. |

There are four power exposures, not four large power workouts. Wednesday throws add 12 normal work throws plus two rehearsal throws; Sunday cleans add eight work catches plus four rehearsal catches; Monday swings add ten work reps plus five rehearsal reps. Friday W2–4 adds four clean reps plus two rehearsal reps. Rehearsal stress is real even though it receives no work-set floor credit. Friday jump stress is counted in the impact ledger only. Carries are one easy walk/pair each, two days weekly; their grip demand is still logged.

Strength floors are planning constraints, not proof that every set is beneficial. If new work disrupts priority performance, its low nominal set count does not exempt it from reduction.

## 9. OHP calibration and meaningful loading

## 13. Running progression and the 15/30-minute limits

No running in W1 or W12. Terrain, distance and effort do not advance together. If an exposure is skipped, symptomatic, or reduced below the stage, it does not qualify that stage. Repeat rather than catch up. Maximum prescribed acceleration is 60 m/session, 120 including runout, below the 250 m original ceiling by either convention. No 85–95% running stage is added this block.

Walk/easy return occurs within recovery only if the full easy recovery is preserved. Hill preparation uses a gentle 3×20 s squeeze, 20 s rests, and measured clear route/runout. ## 17. Autoregulation, deloads and practical substitutions

All main-lift numbers are conditional nominal loads. If a first set is about 1 RIR harder than prescribed: OHP/dip/pull-up −2.5 lb, bench/squat −5 lb, DL −10 lb. Use a short domain review for global recovery, push, pull, lower, elastic and running: normal = comparable performance and ordinary symptoms; caution = unusual fatigue/decline or familiar mild soreness; stop affected work = the symptom triggers in section 14, or performance/technique that cannot stay within the prescribed reserve. No wearable scores or manufactured numeric readiness formula.

Level 1: one poor exposure—hold progression and reduce an affected final set if necessary. Level 2: repeated comparable decline or accumulating local fatigue—reduce affected-family sets approximately 25–35%, preserving safe familiar frequency; review ride/impact/new-accessory overlap. If only lower days decline, do not automatically deload healthy upper priorities. Level 3: persistent multi-domain decline or substantial symptoms—review 5–7 easy days at roughly half sets and ≥4 RIR, with no high/fast impact. Symptom-based stopping never waits for permission. An unscheduled deload only replaces the scheduled one when within ten days and reviewed.
